Frequently Asked Questions

What is an HSA and who is eligible?

A Health Savings Account (HSA) lets you save pre-tax money for qualified medical expenses. To be eligible, you must be enrolled in a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) — in 2024, that means a deductible of at least $1,600 (self-only) or $3,200 (family). You cannot be enrolled in Medicare or claimed as a dependent.

What are the triple tax benefits of an HSA?

HSAs offer three tax advantages: (1) Contributions are pre-tax or tax-deductible, reducing your taxable income. (2) Investment growth is tax-free. (3) Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. No other account type offers all three simultaneously.

Can I invest my HSA funds?

Yes — most HSA providers allow you to invest your balance once it exceeds a threshold (typically $1,000–$2,000). Many financial advisors recommend a "pay out of pocket now, invest and grow, reimburse yourself later" strategy to maximise investment time.

What happens to unused HSA funds?

Unlike FSAs, HSA funds roll over indefinitely — there is no use-it-or-lose-it rule. After age 65, you can withdraw for any reason (like a traditional IRA), but non-medical withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income.

What qualifies as an HSA medical expense?

Qualified expenses include deductibles, copays, prescriptions, dental, vision, mental health care, and many more. See IRS Publication 502 for the full list. Non-qualified withdrawals before age 65 incur a 20% penalty plus income tax.

How it works

An HSA grows like any compounding account: FV = P·((1+r)^n − 1) ÷ r for regular annual contributions P at return rate r over n years. On top of growth, contributions reduce your taxable income now, so your immediate tax saving is roughly contribution × marginal tax rate. Because qualified withdrawals are also tax-free, the effective benefit is larger than a comparable taxable account.

Example 1: Contributing $4,000/year at a 6% return for 20 years grows to about $147,000, of which roughly $67,000 is investment gain.
Example 2: A $4,000 contribution for someone in a 24% tax bracket saves about $960 in income tax in the year it is made.

FAQ

What is an HSA and who is eligible?

A Health Savings Account is a tax-advantaged account for people enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP). You cannot be enrolled in other disqualifying coverage or claimed as a dependent.

What are the triple tax benefits of an HSA?

Contributions are tax-deductible, the money grows tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are also tax-free — a rare triple advantage.

Can I invest my HSA funds?

Many HSA providers let you invest the balance above a minimum cash threshold in funds, so unused money can grow over time much like a retirement account.

What happens to unused HSA funds?

Unlike a flexible spending account, HSA funds roll over every year and stay yours, even if you change jobs or health plans.

What qualifies as an HSA medical expense?

Doctor visits, prescriptions, dental, vision, and many other IRS-qualified medical costs. Non-qualified withdrawals before age 65 face income tax plus a penalty.
